The first decision on an Asteria site is not a typeface. It is the paper.

`#f6f8f3` is not white. It is a slightly botanical ground, closer to a sheet that has lived on a desk than to a default browser canvas. Ink (`#101418`) sits on it without glare. Together they do most of the work: long reading, quiet UI, a header that can recede.

The other three colors are employed, not displayed.

- **Teal** (`#177c75`) marks wayfinding — section labels, focus rings, the confirmation that a brief arrived.
- **Coral** (`#d94f42`) is the only loud action. If two things on a screen are coral, one of them is unemployed.
- **Gold** (`#c58a22`) is emphasis with manners: an eyebrow over a hero, a date in the journal, a rule under a pull quote.

Sky (`#3667b1`) is the spare room. We use it when a composition needs a cooler counterweight, never as a substitute for teal.

## Night is the same house

A dark theme that invents a second brand is a different website wearing the same name. Ours keeps the rooms and lowers the lights. Paper becomes a deep olive-black. Ink becomes the old paper. Teal and coral lighten just enough to keep contrast honest. The brand-mark — that small gradient square — does not change jobs.

We default to `prefers-color-scheme` and then remember what you chose. The toggle is a control, not a novelty. If you asked for less motion, we do not fade the theme change across a choreographed second. The page simply becomes the other hour of the day.

New hues have to apply for a role. Purple gradients, glassmorphism, and the seasonal accent of whatever dashboard library is shipping this quarter are not in the building. Color, here, is architecture: load-bearing, limited, and meant to be inherited.
