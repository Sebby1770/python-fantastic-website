The worst briefs we receive are the longest. They arrive as decks that describe a "holistic omnichannel presence" and never name the person who has to say yes. The best briefs fit in the form on this site: a name, a mail address, the shape of the work, a budget band, a horizon, and a paragraph that sounds like a human.

That is enough to know whether we can help.

## What we actually read

**Audience.** Who is this for on a Tuesday, not in a persona workshop. A partner at a consulting firm. A buyer who will forward one URL. A reader who wants the next Harbor Press title, not a storefront.

**Offer.** The sentence you would say if the site went down and you had to explain the practice at a table. If you cannot say it, we will spend the first week writing, not designing.

**Proof.** Two or three things that happened. Not adjectives. Northline did not need to look "innovative." They needed the work to look as careful as the advice.

**Constraint.** Budget and timeline are not vulgar. They keep us from proposing a catalog when you needed a landing page in six weeks. "Still exploring" is an honest answer. "Make it world-class" is not a constraint.

## Why the form asks what it asks

The extra fields on `/contact` are optional because some people are not ready to name a number. They are there because when they are ready, we should not have to schedule a call to learn it. The endpoint validates, stores the row in SQLite, and answers immediately. Nothing is mailed into a void. The studio inbox is a page, token-protected, on purpose: inquiries are correspondence, not leads to be drip-fed.

If you are mid-search and only have a paragraph, send the paragraph. We would rather read a short true note than a long document that is afraid to choose.
